## 1. The $SL_2$-Equivariant Symmetric Power Framework

Terence Tao's geometric digestion of the 3D counterexample uses the multiplication of linear and quadratic homogeneous polynomials of two complex variables. Let:
- $L(z,w) = az + bw \in \mathrm{Sym}^1(\mathbb{C}^2)$
- $Q(z,w) = cz^2 + dzw + ew^2 \in \mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$

The product of these is a cubic polynomial:
- $C(z,w) = fz^3 + gz^2w + hzw^2 + iw^3 \in \mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathbb{C}^2)$

The multiplication map $F(L, Q) = LQ$ maps $\mathbb{C}^5 \to \mathbb{C}^4$:
$$ (a,b,c,d,e) \mapsto (ac, ad + bc, ae + bd, be) $$

To eliminate the scaling symmetry $(L, Q) \mapsto (\lambda L, \lambda^{-1} Q)$ and restrict to a 3-dimensional affine variety, we impose:
- $\mathrm{Res}(L, Q) = 1 \implies a^2 e - abd + cb^2 = 1$
- The affine hyperplane section $g = ad + bc = 1$

Through polynomial change of variables, this variety is globally parameterized by three coordinates $(a, y, z)$ via:
$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= a \\
b &= 1 + ay \\
c &= 1 - \frac{3}{2}ay + a^2z \\
d &= \frac{1}{2}y - az + \frac{3}{2}ay^2 - a^2yz \\
e &= -2z + 4y^2 - 4ayz + 3ay^3 - 2a^2y^2z
\end{aligned}
$$

The mapping to the coefficients $(f, h, i) \equiv (G_1, G_2, G_3)$ of the product polynomial $LQ$ is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_1(a,y,z) &= a - \frac{3}{2}a^2y + a^3z \\
G_2(a,y,z) &= \frac{1}{2}y - 3az + 6ay^2 - 6a^2yz + \frac{9}{2}a^2y^3 - 3a^3y^2z \\
G_3(a,y,z) &= -2z + 4y^2 - 6ayz + 7ay^3 - 6a^2y^2z + 3a^2y^4 - 2a^3y^3z
\end{aligned}
$$

The 3D Jacobian determinant with respect to $(a, y, z)$ is exactly the constant:
$$ \det J(G_1, G_2, G_3) = -1 $$

This represents a beautifully normalized version of the original 3D counterexample.

## 3. Flat Source-Plane Slice Restriction ($a = c$)

With the $SL_2$-equivariant coordinates fully established, we tested whether restricting the source coordinate $a$ to a constant value $a = c$ could yield a valid 2D counterexample map $(G_2|_{a=c}, G_3|_{a=c})$.

### 3.1 2D Jacobian Determinant w.r.t $(y, z)$
The restricted components on the $a=c$ slice are:
$$ G_2(y, z) = -3c^3 y^2 z + \frac{9}{2} c^2 y^3 - 6c^2 y z + 6c y^2 - 3c z + \frac{1}{2}y $$
$$ G_3(y, z) = -2c^3 y^3 z + 3c^2 y^4 - 6c^2 y^2 z + 7c y^3 - 6c y z + 4y^2 - 2z $$

Computing the restricted 2D Jacobian determinant w.r.t $(y, z)$ yields:
$$ \operatorname{Jac}_{y,z}(G_2, G_3) = -6 c^6 y^4 z + 9 c^5 y^5 - 24 c^5 y^3 z + 30 c^4 y^4 - 36 c^4 y^2 z + 32 c^3 y^3 - 24 c^3 y z + 9 c^2 y^2 - 6 c^2 z - 3 c y - 1 $$

### Conclusion:
$$\boxed{\operatorname{Jac}_{y,z}(G_2|_{a=c}, G_3|_{a=c}) \neq \text{constant}}$$
Because the Jacobian is a non-constant polynomial of high degree in $(y, z)$, **no flat source slice $a = c$ can ever produce a constant Jacobian 2D map.**
